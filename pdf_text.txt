Background 
MetroHealth System is a major healthcare provider experiencing a surge in daily patient visits 
across its network. Each day, thousands of patients enter different facility types to seek 
consultation, treatment, emergency care, or routine health screenings.  Concerned about 
waiting times, bed allocation, and operational bottlenecks, the hospital board wishes to 
optimize healthcare resource management. To better understand patient flow and resource 
usage, detailed registration and clinical data were collected across three distinct facilities: 
 
1. Facility A (General Hospital - Metropolitan Hub): 
• Adult and elderly patients admitted or visited for outpatient consultations, 
emergency care, surgical procedures, or routine check-ups.  
2. Facility B (University Medical Center): 
• Young adults, students, and pediatric patients undergoing preventive health 
screenings, minor treatments, vaccination drives, or sports injury rehab.  
3. Facility C (Community Health Clinic - Rural/Suburban): 
• Residents of all ages seeking general practitioner consultations, chronic disease 
monitoring, and maternal care. 
 
The dataset collected for each patient includes: 
1. Patient ID - A unique identifier for each patient (example: PT10042).  
2. Age - The age of the patient.  
3. Care Type - Type of healthcare service received (Emergency, Outpatient, Inpatient, 
Vaccination, Rehabilitation, Routine Checkup).  
4. Length of Stay / Visit Duration (Hours) - The total hours spent in the facility.  
5. Base Cost Per Hour (MYR/hr) - The standard billing rate associated with the 
specific care type.  
6. Days Visits Per Year - The average number of visits or days per year the patient 
utilizes hospital services.  
 
Hospital administrators aim to analyze this data to identify high -cost care departments, 
compare medical service utilization across facilities, and design policies that reduce patient 
wait times while optimizing medical resource distribution.   
To perform this analysis efficiently, the hospital’s IT research team decided to store and process 
the dataset using arrays and singly linked lists, applying various sorting and searching 
algorithms. By comparing the performance of these fundamental data s tructures and 
algorithms, the administration can determine the most effective methods to manage large 
medical records while gaining actionable clinical insights.
